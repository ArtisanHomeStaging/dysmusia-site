Books

An annotated starting library — what each book covers, and who it's actually useful for.

Edited volume · Case studies & research

Music and Dyslexia: Opening New Doors

Editors: T. R. Miles & John Westcombe. Publisher: Whurr, 2001.
Useful for: teachers and specialists wanting the closest thing to a field reference.
Focus: a collected volume of case studies and research papers, including Margaret Hubicki's writing on multisensory notation teaching.
Why it's here: it's one of the few book-length treatments dedicated specifically to music and dyslexia, gathering multiple specialists' work in one place.

Guide · Teachers

Teacher Guide to Music and Dyslexia

Publisher: British Dyslexia Association, with Music Teacher Magazine.
Useful for: classroom and studio music teachers.
Focus: practical guidance for recognizing and accommodating notation-reading difficulty in lessons.
Why it's here: it's free, teacher-facing, and written specifically for the classroom rather than a research audience — see the Links page for where to find it.

Article & case study

Music and Dyslexia — and How Suzuki Helps

Author: Jenny Macmillan.
Useful for: instrument teachers considering ear-first methods.
Focus: how the Suzuki method's ear-before-notation sequencing benefits dyslexic students, plus a review of the (small) surrounding research literature.
Why it's here: it doubles as a short literature review, referencing most of the field's core papers in one place.

General audience

Dyslexia & Music: general reading

Useful for: parents and adult learners wanting an accessible overview.
Focus: plain-language explanations of the dyslexia–music connection and general multisensory strategies for learning an instrument.
Why it's here: a lower-barrier entry point before diving into specialist or academic material.

Dysmusia

An independent, informational project exploring why some capable musicians struggle to read music — and finding better ways to teach them. Not a diagnostic tool or medical advice.
