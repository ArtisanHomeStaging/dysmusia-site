About the Project

Exploring why some capable musicians struggle to read music — and finding better ways to teach them.

The Dysmusia Project exists because a specific, recognizable pattern — strong musicality paired with real, persistent difficulty reading notation — doesn't have anywhere near the resources, language, or research attention that its cousin, dyslexia, has. Teachers and parents encounter it constantly. Students live with it. And yet the term itself is barely a generation old, and the science is still catching up.

This site is an attempt to bring together what's currently known — from neuroscience, music education, and the accumulated experience of specialist teachers — into one place that's honest about what's established and what isn't. It leads with the plainest possible explanations, avoids overclaiming a formal diagnosis that doesn't exist yet, and tries to give students, parents, and teachers something practical to act on: names for the experience, strategies that have worked for others, and a growing, annotated set of resources for going deeper.

It's an ongoing project. New pages, activities, research summaries, and materials are being added regularly.
