Research Articles

The primary literature this site draws on. Plain-language breakdowns of several of these live on the Research Summaries page.

Research specifically on music-reading difficulty is thin compared to the literature on dyslexia, but a real, if scattered, body of work exists — spanning neuroscience, music education, and case-study reports from specialists working directly with students. A few frequently cited threads:

  • Ayotte and Peretz's work at the University of Montreal on congenital amusia, which helped establish that people who consider themselves "tone deaf" can show measurable, consistent deficits in pitch discrimination.
  • Neil Gordon's original 2000 proposal of "musical dyslexia," drawing a direct parallel to dyslexia and dyscalculia research.
  • Case-study and survey work from music-and-dyslexia specialists, including Dr. Elizabeth Morrow's survey of string teachers on the prevalence of notation-reading difficulty in their studios.
  • Margaret Hubicki's writing on multisensory notation teaching, along with the collected case studies and research papers gathered in Music and Dyslexia (T. R. Miles & John Westcombe, eds.).

For accessible summaries of specific findings, see Research Summaries. This page will grow as a running bibliography as more sources are added.
